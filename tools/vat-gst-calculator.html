How VAT and GST are calculated

Adding tax is a multiplication: the tax is the price times the rate, and the total is the price plus the tax. At the UAE's 5%, a price of AED 1,000 carries AED 50 of VAT for a total of AED 1,050. Removing tax from a total that already includes it is a division, not a subtraction: divide the total by one plus the rate. AED 1,050 divided by 1.05 is AED 1,000, so the VAT inside it is AED 50.

The common mistake is to take 5% of the total and subtract it. 5% of 1,050 is 52.50, which would leave 997.50, two and a half dirhams short. A quicker way to find the tax inside a total is a fixed fraction: at 5% the tax is 1/21 of the total, at 15% it is 3/23, and at 18% it is 9/59. The calculator shows that fraction for whichever rate you choose.

All amounts are worked out in whole fils, halalas, baisa or paise, so the net amount plus the tax always equals the total exactly and there are no floating-point errors such as 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004. Tax is rounded half up to the nearest minor unit, or to whole dirhams, riyals or rupees if you tick the rounding option.

In the UAE, some supplies are zero-rated, which means VAT is charged at 0% and the supplier can still recover the VAT on its costs: exports outside the GCC, international transport, certain education and healthcare services, investment-grade precious metals and the first supply of a new residential building. Others are exempt, with no VAT charged and no recovery: most residential rents after the first supply, bare land, local passenger transport and some financial services. A business must register for VAT once its taxable supplies and imports pass AED 375,000 in twelve months, and may register voluntarily from AED 187,500. Check the Federal Tax Authority's guidance for how your own supplies are treated.

GST in India: CGST, SGST and IGST

Indian GST has the same arithmetic but is shared between the central government and the states. On a sale within one state, half the rate is charged as Central GST and half as State GST, so 18% appears on the invoice as CGST 9% and SGST 9%. On a sale from one state to another, or on an import, the full rate is charged as Integrated GST. In union territories without their own legislature, such as Chandigarh and Ladakh, Union Territory GST replaces SGST. For ₹10,000 at 18% within Maharashtra the invoice shows CGST ₹900 and SGST ₹900; from Maharashtra to Karnataka it shows IGST ₹1,800. The total is ₹11,800 either way.

From 22 September 2025 most goods and services moved to two main rates, 5% and 18%, with 40% on a short list of luxury and sin goods, replacing the earlier 12% and 28% slabs. Special rates continue for some goods, such as 3% on gold, silver and jewellery and 0.25% on rough diamonds. The older rates remain in the list for invoices dated before the change. The correct rate depends on the HSN code of the goods or the SAC code of the service, so confirm it in the current rate notifications rather than relying on a general list. When GST is paid, the amount is rounded to the nearest rupee, which the whole-units option reproduces.

Amount in words

Invoices, cheques and contracts often print the total in words as well as figures, so that a figure cannot be altered unnoticed. The calculator writes the total the way each country reads numbers: Indian rupee amounts use lakh and crore, so ₹1,23,456.78 is Rupees One Lakh Twenty-Three Thousand Four Hundred Fifty-Six and Seventy-Eight Paise Only, while dirham, riyal and dinar amounts use thousand and million. Omani rials and Bahraini dinars have three decimal places, 1,000 baisa or fils to the unit, and the calculator keeps all three.
