What is PINT-AE?

PINT-AE is the UAE's e-invoicing data format. Instead of a PDF or Excel invoice that a computer can't reliably read, PINT-AE is a structured XML file — built on the international "Peppol" e-invoicing network — that tax systems and accounting software can process automatically.

The UAE Federal Tax Authority (FTA) has published technical guidance requiring businesses to eventually issue invoices in this format instead of plain PDFs, spreadsheets, or paper.

Do I Need This?

If you invoice customers in the UAE and are VAT-registered, yes — eventually. The rollout is phased, so most small businesses have time to prepare:

  • From July 2026 — voluntary. You can start early to get comfortable.
  • From January 2027 — mandatory for businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more.
  • From July 2027 — mandatory for all other VAT-registered businesses.

Three layers behind the name

"PINT-AE" gets used as though it were a single specification, and separating the layers makes the requirements much clearer:

  • UBL 2.1 — the underlying XML grammar, an OASIS standard defining <Invoice>, <InvoiceLine>, <TaxTotal> and the rest. Two namespaces do the work: cbc for simple values, cac for composite structures.
  • Peppol PINT — OpenPeppol's international invoice specification: which UBL elements are mandatory, what they mean, and the business rules that must hold.
  • PINT-AE — the UAE profile of PINT, adding local identifiers, local tax categories and the Federal Tax Authority's own requirements.

The consequence is that a document can be perfectly valid UBL and still fail PINT-AE validation, which is where most first integration attempts land. Our UAE e-invoicing guide covers the five-corner exchange model and the readiness work in detail.

Totals must reconcile

The sum of line amounts must equal the line extension total, which must tie to the tax-exclusive amount, the tax total and the payable amount. Every arithmetic relationship between them is a rule, and a discrepancy of a few fils fails validation where a PDF would have displayed it happily.

Tax categories are distinct

Standard-rated, zero-rated, exempt, out-of-scope and reverse-charge are separate categories with separate codes. Zero-rated and exempt are not interchangeable, and a catalogue that stores "5%" as a number cannot express the difference at all.

Unit codes come from a list

unitCode values are drawn from UN/ECE Recommendation 20. EA, HUR and KGM are valid; each, hrs and pcs are not, however reasonable they look.

It is a data project, not an XML project

Teams usually budget for the integration — build the XML, connect a provider, test, done — and the integration turns out to be the small part. What consumes the timeline is discovering that a third of your customer records have no usable tax identifier, that addresses are stored as one free-text line where the rules expect discrete city and country components, and that totals have been rounding in two different places for years.

None of that cleanup depends on a confirmed deadline, which is the argument for starting now: audit counterparty master data, classify every product and service with a tax category and a valid unit code, make rounding consistent in one place, and confirm invoice numbers are genuinely unique. Then export your ten most complicated real invoices — foreign currency, mixed tax categories, credit notes, partial discounts — and try to express each one. Your edge cases are not the specification's examples, and that exercise finds the real gaps faster than any amount of reading.

What this tool is and is not

This is a readiness and learning aid. It checks invoice data against the PINT-AE field structure, shows what is missing or inconsistent, and exports a PINT-AE style XML file so you can see the shape of the document your systems will need to produce. It runs entirely in your browser, so nothing you enter is transmitted.

It is not an FTA-approved filing route, and it cannot be: under the UAE model, invoices are exchanged and reported through an Accredited Service Provider, not submitted directly. Treat output from here as a draft for inspection. Have your ASP or a qualified tax adviser verify anything you actually file, and confirm your own obligations and deadlines with the Federal Tax Authority — phased timelines have been adjusted more than once, and a technical tool is the wrong source for a regulatory date.
