How the days are counted

The difference between two dates is the number of midnights between them. From 1 January to 2 January is one day, and from 1 January to 31 December 2026 is 364 days. That is the right answer for questions like "how long until" or "how old is this invoice", and it matches what spreadsheets return when you subtract one date from another. When you are counting days that are used up, such as days of leave, nights booked or days a contract runs, you usually want both the first and the last day included. Tick Count the end date too and the same range becomes 365 days.

Months and years are counted on the calendar, not as a fixed number of days, because months run from 28 to 31 days. The calculator finds the largest number of whole months that fits between the dates and then counts the days left over, so 15 January to 20 March is 2 months and 5 days. It never uses an average month of 30 or 30.44 days, which is why its answers agree with how people count birthdays and anniversaries.

Working days in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and India

"Working days" means the days that are not weekends or holidays, and the weekend depends on the country. Since January 2022 the UAE works Monday to Friday with a Saturday and Sunday weekend, as most of the world does. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain keep a Friday and Saturday weekend, so a deadline of ten working days lands on a different date in Riyadh than in Dubai. In India many offices, shops and factories work six days with only Sunday off, while most banks also close on the second and fourth Saturday of each month.

Public holidays change every year, and Islamic holidays such as Eid move with the lunar calendar and are confirmed only days in advance. Pick a country to skip its public holidays: the list comes from the open date-holidays data set (version shown under the list), except for Qatar, Kuwait and Oman, which that data set does not cover, where Layerbit applies the holidays set out in each country's labour law. Government decrees often lengthen the Eid holidays at short notice, so add any extra days to the holidays box, one date per line, and the calculator leaves them out of the count. A holiday that falls on a weekend is counted once, as a weekend day. Notice periods, visa validity and legal deadlines often have their own counting rules, so check the wording of the contract or regulation as well.

Adding days or months

Adding days is exact: 90 days from 21 November 2026 is 19 February 2027. Adding months keeps the same day of the month, and when that day does not exist the result moves back to the last day of the shorter month, so 31 January plus one month is 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year. That is why 90 days and 3 months are not the same thing: from 21 November, 3 months later is 21 February. Working days skip weekends and listed holidays, and adding one working day to a Friday gives Monday with a Saturday and Sunday weekend, but Sunday with a Friday and Saturday weekend.

Exact age and leap-day birthdays

Age is counted the way birthdays are: the number of complete years since the date of birth, then the complete months since the last birthday, then the remaining days. Someone born on 15 May 1990 is 36 years, 6 months and 6 days old on 21 November 2026, which is 13,339 days. People born on 29 February have a real birthday only in leap years; in other years the calculator treats 28 February as the birthday, which is the convention most software uses. Some countries' laws use 1 March instead for legal age, so check if the exact day matters.

Leap years and week numbers

A year is a leap year if it divides by 4, except for years that divide by 100, which are leap years only if they also divide by 400. So 2000 and 2024 were leap years, 1900 was not, and 2026 is not. Week numbers follow ISO 8601, used across Europe and by most business software: weeks start on Monday, and week 1 is the week that contains the year's first Thursday. That is why 1 January 2027, a Friday, belongs to week 53 of 2026.
