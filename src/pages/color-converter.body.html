Which color format to use

Every format on the left describes the same color; they differ in what is easy to read and edit. Hex and rgb() are the universal interchange formats: every design tool, browser and platform understands them, which is why they are the safe choice for brand guidelines and design tokens. hsl() was the first attempt at a human-editable format, with hue, saturation and lightness, but its lightness is not perceptual: hsl(60 100% 50%) yellow and hsl(240 100% 50%) blue have the same L yet look wildly different in brightness.

oklch() fixes that. Its L is perceived lightness, C is chroma (colorfulness) and H is hue, in a space designed so that equal numeric steps look like equal visual steps. Changing L in OKLCH changes brightness without shifting the hue, which makes it the best format for building scales, hover states and dark-mode variants. Every current browser supports oklch() in CSS. lab() and lch() are the older CIE equivalents, used in print and color science. cmyk() here is a naive conversion for quick reference; real print output depends on the press's ICC profile.

The WCAG contrast rules

WCAG 2.2 measures contrast as a ratio between the relative luminance of the lighter and darker colors, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white). The thresholds depend on what the color is used for:

CriterionApplies toMinimum ratio
1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), level AABody text and images of text4.5:1
1.4.3, level AALarge text: 24px regular, or 18.66px (14pt) bold and up3:1
1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced), level AAABody text7:1
1.4.6, level AAALarge text4.5:1
1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, level AAIcons, input borders, focus rings, chart lines3:1

The ratio is truncated, never rounded up: #777777 on white is 4.47:1, and that famous near miss fails AA even though it looks fine to many people. Transparent text is composited onto the background first, so a 60% opacity grey is judged on the color you actually see. Placeholder text, disabled controls and logos are exempt; everything else a user must read is not.

What APCA adds

The Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm, developed for the next generation of guidelines, models how contrast is actually perceived. Unlike the WCAG 2 ratio, it is polarity-aware (light text on dark is treated differently from dark on light) and far more accurate for mid-tones and dark themes, where the WCAG 2 formula is known to pass pairs that are hard to read and fail pairs that are fine. APCA reports a lightness contrast value, Lc, from 0 to about 106. As a rule of thumb, Lc 90 suits long body text, Lc 75 is the minimum for body text, Lc 60 for content text larger than body size, Lc 45 for large headlines, and Lc 30 for placeholder and disabled text. APCA is not yet a legal standard, so check both: WCAG 2 for compliance, APCA for whether people can comfortably read it.

How the contrast fix works

The fix buttons keep the text color's hue and chroma and move only its OKLCH lightness, searching for the value closest to the original that reaches the target ratio against the current background. The result stays recognizably the same color, just darker or lighter, instead of being replaced by black or white. If no lightness can reach the target, for example a saturated mid-tone background that sits too close to the middle of the range, the tool falls back to black or white, whichever contrasts more. The tint and shade scale below uses the same space, so the 600 or 700 step of your brand color is often already a compliant text color on white.

Designing for color-vision deficiencies

About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a color-vision deficiency, most often deuteranomaly or protanomaly, which reduce the ability to tell red from green. The simulations use the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) model at full severity for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, plus full achromatopsia, and recompute the contrast ratio for each. Contrast based on luminance survives most deficiencies, which is exactly why WCAG measures luminance; the real risk is relying on hue alone. Never use color as the only way to convey meaning: pair a red error state with an icon and text, and give chart series different shapes or line styles as well as colors.

Frequently asked questions

Split the six hex digits into three pairs and convert each pair from base 16 to base 10: #38BDF8 becomes 38, BD and F8, which are 56, 189 and 248, so rgb(56 189 248). A three-digit hex like #0AF doubles each digit first, giving #00AAFF. An eight-digit hex adds an alpha pair, where 80 is about 50% opacity. WCAG 2.2 level AA, the level most laws and policies reference, requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, meaning 24px regular or 18.66px bold and larger. Level AAA raises these to 7:1 and 4.5:1. User-interface components and meaningful graphics such as icons, input borders and focus indicators need 3:1 against adjacent colors. OKLCH is a color format built on the OKLab color space, published by Björn Ottosson in 2020. Its three values are perceived lightness, chroma and hue. Because lightness is perceptual, two colors with the same L look equally bright, so you can generate shade scales, hover states and dark themes by changing one number without hues drifting or yellows looking washed out. It is supported in CSS by every current browser and can express colors outside sRGB on wide-gamut displays.

OKLCH, LCH and Lab can describe colors more saturated than an sRGB screen can show, for example oklch(70% 0.35 150). When that happens the tool maps the color into sRGB the way CSS Color 4 specifies: it keeps the lightness and hue and reduces chroma until the color fits, rather than clipping each channel, which would shift the hue. The hex and rgb values shown are that mapped color; on a wide-gamut display the original may look more vivid in a browser.

APCA predicts readability more accurately, especially for dark themes and mid-tone colors, and accounts for whether text is lighter or darker than its background. But WCAG 2.2's ratio is the standard referenced by accessibility laws today, so a design must still pass it. The practical approach is to meet the WCAG 2 thresholds for compliance and use APCA to catch pairs that technically pass but are uncomfortable to read.
