How time zone conversion works

Every time zone is defined by its offset from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Dubai is UTC+4, India is UTC+5:30 and New York is UTC−5 in winter. To convert a time, you subtract the first zone's offset to get UTC and add the second zone's offset. 3:00 PM in Dubai is 11:00 UTC, which is 4:30 PM in India and 6:00 AM in New York. The converter does this for every city at once, and shows when the result falls on the previous or next day.

The hard part is that offsets are not fixed. Many countries move their clocks forward an hour in summer, on dates that differ between countries, so the gap between two cities can change several times a year. This tool does not use a hand-made table of offsets. It asks your browser's built-in copy of the IANA time zone database, the same data used by phones, operating systems and servers, for the offset in force on the exact date you choose. That is how it knows that India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of New York in January but 9 hours 30 minutes ahead in July.

Daylight saving: why the difference changes

India, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Pakistan, Singapore, China and Japan do not change their clocks, so the gap between any two of them is the same all year. The United States, Canada, the UK and most of Europe do, and so do Sydney and Melbourne in the opposite season. In 2026 the US moves forward on 8 March and back on 1 November; the UK and the EU move forward on 29 March and back on 25 October; Sydney moves back on 5 April and forward on 4 October.

Because the US and Europe switch on different Sundays, the London to New York gap is four hours instead of five for about three weeks in March and one week around the end of October. Recurring meetings between the two drift by an hour in those weeks, which catches out calendars that store a fixed offset instead of a zone. Pick a date in one of those weeks in the converter to see it.

On the night clocks go forward, an hour of local time never happens: in New York, 2:30 AM on 8 March 2026 does not exist. On the night they go back, an hour happens twice. If you enter one of those times, the converter says so, shows how it was interpreted, and gives the second occurrence of a repeated time.

Finding a meeting time across zones

The planner shows every hour of the chosen day in the first city, next to the local time in each other city, and highlights the hours that fall inside 09:00 to 18:00 everywhere. A few patterns worth knowing:

  • India and the US East Coast: there is almost no overlap in office hours. US mornings, 8:00 to 10:00 AM in New York, are 6:30 to 8:30 PM in India in winter, so calls usually happen in the Indian evening.
  • Dubai and India: almost a full shared day, since India is only an hour and a half ahead. Meetings starting between 9:00 AM and 3:00 PM Dubai time fit both offices.
  • Dubai or India and London: London's morning is the Gulf and India's afternoon, so 9:00 AM in London is 1:00 PM in Dubai and 2:30 PM in India in winter.
  • The Gulf work week: the UAE works Monday to Friday, but Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait and Oman take Friday and Saturday off, so check the day as well as the hour.
